Change management has a metaphor problem. Most frameworks reach for icebergs,
stages of grief, or freeze-unfreeze diagrams. Useful, abstract, hard to feel.

A juggler reaches for the bag. You can put a ball in someone's hand and they
will know within ninety seconds what it feels like to add load to a system
that was working fine a moment ago.

## Why juggling translates

Change leadership and juggling share an awkward truth: the limiting factor is
rarely the new thing. It's whether the existing pattern stays smooth while
the new thing is being added.

## What a workshop looks like

A typical Juggling Company change-management session has three blocks:

1. **Add load** — Everyone learns one ball, then two, then three. Failures are
   public and harmless. The room reframes "drop" from "failure" to "data".
2. **Pattern recognition** — We swap props. LED balls. Different weights.
   Sometimes we use [AI-based object detection][cv] to overlay trajectory
   predictions on a wall — making the brain's hidden tracking work visible.
3. **Translate** — Small groups map their juggling experience onto a real change
   they're leading. The mapping is rarely the obvious one.

<Callout type="success" title="Why this works for teams">
Nobody has to be vulnerable about the change they're scared of. They have to
be vulnerable about dropping a ball. The transfer happens after, in the
debrief — quieter, but it sticks.
</Callout>

## What you take home

- A reframe of "drop" as recoverable, not catastrophic.
- A felt sense for incremental load — and the moment a system can't take more.
- A vocabulary your team can use mid-meeting: _"we're past three balls"_,
  _"slow the throws"_, _"watch the apex"_.
